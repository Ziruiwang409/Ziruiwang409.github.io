Complete3D — Completion from a Partial Laser Scan on ScanNet++

Model = Complete3D-v2 (100k) · merged = the scan kept verbatim + the prediction only where the scan saw nothing · 137 test scenes · GT = the full laser mesh · protocol as the image-only benchmark · scan-conditioned experiment → · navigation application →
Two settings, different inputs. Section 1 uses the scan + 8 DSLR images: the first laser station only (82% of the GT surface). Section 2 uses the scan alone, no images: the first 50 % of the stations (94% of the GT surface). At that coverage the merge does not beat the input scan on CD; it adds the missing surface but less accurately than laser data.

Merge rule (both sections)

  • Free space = 2 cm voxels crossed by each laser ray (station → point), stopping 4 cm before the hit.
  • A predicted vertex is dropped if it lies in free space (the scan saw nothing there) or within 3 cm of a scan point (the scan already has the surface); a predicted face survives only if all three vertices do. Merged = scan mesh ∪ surviving predicted faces.
  • Leakage check: the share of the raw predicted surface that lies in space the scan saw empty (removed by the rule) — 7.9% in section 1, 11.8% in section 2 (mean over scenes); predicted faces kept on average 72% and 63%.

2 · First 50 % of laser stations, scan only (no images)

Input = the first half of the laser stations (94% of the GT surface) and nothing else: no images at any stage (stage 1 scan-only, stages 2–3 unconditional) · 2026-10-15 · 137 test scenes

With half the stations the scan already covers 94% of the GT, and here the merge does not beat the input scan on CD (0.0267 input vs 0.0389 merged). It does improve on the raw prediction (0.0518 → 0.0389) and completes the scan (completeness 0.0410 → 0.0246, recall 0.924 → 0.982), but the generated surface it adds is far less accurate than laser data (accuracy 0.0124 → 0.0532), which outweighs the completeness gained.
